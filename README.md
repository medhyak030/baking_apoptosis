# baking_apoptosis

### This repository follows a versioned research framework. Major revisions to the conceptual architecture, hypotheses, or project design are documented as new versions, while incremental progress is tracked through Git commits.


### Central theme of the project:
Apoptosis is governed by an integrated regulatory architecture.
This project maps that architecture and identifies the major control levers through which ligand-based modulation could, in principle, influence cell fate.

### Why this project?
A cell's fate of live or death is governed by a cluster of regulatory systems.

Why does a cell choose apoptosis?
Why would it kill itself?
How it may influence other cells surrounding it?
Does the genomic sequence play a role in death?
Would increasing a telomere's length change it's fate?
Do mutations in any way decide survival? or maybe artificially creating them stop the cell from shutting off?
How would the genomic sequences of different species regulate this architecture?
Who actually does receive a signal of death?
Would a mutated ligand stop death/survival from happening and how it might regulate it?
Is cell's fate determined as soon as it's duplicated?
I think telomerase could change certain things;

These are a thread of statements I encountered initially when I developed an interest in the apoptopic pathway. Learning about the whole architecture and activations it takes for a cell to decide it's fate intrigued me to question further.
