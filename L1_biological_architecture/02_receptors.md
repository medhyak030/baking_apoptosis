# 02 Receptor Architecture and Cellular Gatekeeping


### "The cell is a gatekeeper, TRIAL a messenger"

## Research Question

How does the receptor landscape determine whether TRAIL signalling results in apoptosis or cellular survival?

## Background
Following the identification of TRAIL and its receptor classes, the next objective was to understand how receptor architecture influences signalling outcomes. Initial observations suggested that receptor identity alone could not fully explain the dual behaviour of TRAIL, prompting further investigation into receptor structure, regulation, and signalling competence.

### New findings:

This stage of the project extended the understanding of TRAIL beyond its primary receptor system and introduced several regulatory concepts that significantly changed the direction of the investigation.

- Identification of a fifth TRAIL-binding receptor, Osteoprotegerin (OPG), expanding the receptor system beyond DR4, DR5, DcR1 and DcR2.
- Recognition that DR5 functions as the principal execution receptor, whereas DR4 appears to play a more variable and often supplementary role.
- Discovery that receptor behaviour is influenced not only by ligand binding but also by receptor expression, membrane organisation and cellular state.
- Identification of TRA8, a monoclonal antibody capable of restoring apoptotic signalling by promoting productive DR5 activation.
- Recognition that p53 regulates both death receptors and decoy receptors, indicating that its function extends beyond simply promoting apoptosis.
- Realisation that TRAIL itself does not determine signalling outcome; instead, the cellular environment constructs the receptor landscape that TRAIL encounters.

These observations shifted the focus of the project from receptor identification toward understanding the regulatory mechanisms governing receptor behaviour.


# Current Understanding

### Death Receptors

#### DR4 (TRAIL-R1)

- First identified TRAIL death receptor.
- Expression is relatively variable across tissues.
- Generally plays a supplementary role during stress-induced apoptosis.
- Participates in apoptotic signalling following productive TRAIL binding.

#### DR5 (TRAIL-R2)

- Principal execution receptor for TRAIL-mediated apoptosis.
- Frequently upregulated following severe cellular stress.
- Promotes efficient DISC formation and activation of the extrinsic apoptotic pathway.
- Serves as the dominant execution receptor under many apoptotic conditions.


### Decoy Receptors

#### DcR1 (TRAIL-R3)

- GPI-anchored membrane receptor.
- Lacks an intracellular cytoplasmic signalling domain.
- Cannot recruit signalling proteins.
- Functions primarily by sequestering TRAIL and preventing productive clustering of DR4 and DR5.

#### DcR2 (TRAIL-R4)

- Possesses a cytoplasmic region but contains a truncated, non-functional death domain.
- Unable to assemble a functional DISC.
- Activates NF-κB-dependent signalling pathways. ( Research Continuity:
One of the unresolved questions from Layer 1 was how DcR2, despite lacking a functional death domain, activates NF-κB signalling. This observation motivated a broader investigation into the intracellular signalling pathways that determine cellular fate following TRAIL receptor engagement. )
- Promotes transcription of genes associated with cellular survival.

Unlike DcR1, which passively blocks ligand availability, DcR2 actively redirects signalling toward survival-associated pathways.


### OPG! (Osteoprotegerin) 

A fifth TRAIL-binding receptor identified during this stage of the project.

Current understanding:

- Soluble decoy receptor.
- Binds both TRAIL and RANKL.
- Functions primarily as a network modulator rather than a classical signalling receptor.
- Represents a point of communication between apoptotic and survival signalling pathways.


### Regulation by p53
Found a contradiction:
If p53 promotes apoptosis, why does it also increase decoy receptor expression?

Current interpretation:

### Moderate DNA Damage
- Increased expression of decoy receptors.
- Temporary suppression of apoptosis.
- Allows time for DNA repair.

### Severe DNA Damage
- Strong induction of DR5.
- Death receptor signalling overwhelms decoy-mediated inhibition.
- Apoptotic commitment becomes irreversible.

Therefore, p53 functions as a genome-protection controller rather than a simple apoptotic switch.

### What is TRA8?
TRA8 is a monoclonal antibody which might basically antagonize the DR5 in case of synovial fibroblasts.

- TRAIL itself does not decide where it binds.
TRA8 is a monoclonal antibody that:
⦁binds DR5
⦁induces clustering
⦁forces apoptotic signaling even when the cell normally resists
So TRA8 is a designed perturbation of the control system — a perfect example of conceptual ligand modulation in action.

### Regulatory Model
The observations collected during this stage suggest that apoptotic outcome is determined through dynamic interactions between extracellular ligand availability and intracellular regulatory mechanisms.

The signalling process can be represented conceptually as:

Cell state & stress level<
Regulation of receptor expression
(DR4 / DR5 vs DcR1 / DcR2)<
Membrane organisation & receptor clustering<
Effective receptor landscape encountered by TRAIL<
Dominant downstream signalling pathway<
Apoptosis or Cellular Survival

This model indicates that TRAIL functions as a signalling messenger rather than an autonomous decision-maker.



## Key Conclusion
The decision between apoptosis and survival is determined primarily by the regulatory state of the cell.

The cell controls:

- Death receptor abundance.
- Decoy receptor abundance.
- Receptor organisation.
- Downstream signalling competence.

TRAIL simply interacts with the molecular environment presented to it.

This led to one of the central concepts of the project:

> **The cell is the gatekeeper; TRAIL is the messenger.**

The receptor landscape therefore represents the first level of cellular gatekeeping before downstream signalling is initiated.

## Open Questions

Although receptor architecture is now substantially clearer, several important questions remain.

The findings from this stage generated several new research questions.

1. Why would p53 upregulate decoy receptors if its role is to eliminate damaged cells?
2. How are DR4 and DR5 functionally distinguished during apoptosis?
3. What physiological role does OPG play within the TRAIL signalling network?
4. Besides p53, which signalling pathways regulate receptor expression?
5. What structural or genetic mechanisms determine receptor selection?
6. How does receptor organisation influence downstream signalling?
7. How do immune cells, TRAIL and receptor expression interact in vivo?
8. How does DcR2 redirect TRAIL signalling toward NF-κB-mediated survival rather than apoptotic execution?


## Working Hypothesis
The outcome of TRAIL signalling is governed by the regulatory architecture established by the target cell. Cellular state, receptor expression, membrane organisation, and downstream signalling collectively determine whether apoptotic or survival pathways become dominant.

## Next Step
Investigate the biological distribution of TRAIL, receptor clustering, and the genetic and sequence-based mechanisms that regulate receptor function and signalling competence.
