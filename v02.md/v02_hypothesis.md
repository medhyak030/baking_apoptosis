### Question 1:
How much of the structural and functional information of the TRAIL receptor system is encoded in the primary protein sequences of DR4, DR5, DcR1, DcR2 and OPG?

The primary sequences of TRAIL receptors contain the structural information required for their distinct biological roles. Therefore, retrieving and comparing the receptor sequences across species should provide the basis for identifying functionally important regions.


## Layer 2

### Question 2: Cross-Species Receptor Equivalence
Can the human, chimpanzee and mouse TRAIL receptor sequences be treated as simple one-to-one equivalents?

### Working Hypothesis
Human and chimpanzee receptor sequences may show relatively direct correspondence, but mouse receptor orthology should not automatically be assumed to follow a one-to-one relationship with the human TNFRSF10 receptor family.

### Interpretation
During sequence retrieval, an important species-level observation emerged.

The mouse TNFRSF10 family does not necessarily contain a separate one-to-one equivalent for every human TNFRSF10 receptor.

The project therefore uses the observed four-human : one-mouse stringent orthological relationship as an important consideration when interpreting the cross-species analysis.

This means that the assumption:
Human TNFRSF10A/DR4 = mouse TNFRSF10A/DR4


## Layer 2

### Question3: Human–Mouse Sequence Assumption
If human and mouse receptors are not always one-to-one orthologues, how should sequence similarity between them be interpreted?

### Working Hypothesis
Sequence similarity between human and mouse proteins should not automatically be interpreted as evidence of strict one-to-one functional equivalence. Differences in orthological relationships must be considered when interpreting conservation and functional correspondence.

### Interpretation
The mouse TNFRSF10 sequence situation is therefore an important limitation of a simple cross-species comparison.


## Layer 2

### Question 4: Functional Regions
Where are the major structural and functional regions of the TRAIL receptor proteins located within their primary sequences?

### Working Hypothesis
The major functional regions of TRAIL receptors should occupy identifiable positions within the primary sequence and should correspond to the receptor's known structural architecture.

### Interpretation
UniProt feature annotations were retrieved and converted into residue-level positional information.

For the receptor architecture examined, relevant features include regions such as:

  - extracellular domains;
  - cysteine-rich regions;
  - transmembrane regions;
  - intracellular regions;
  - death domains where present.
